Recall, then check

Use this sheet after trying to retrieve the rule yourself. The exam is closed book and permits no cheat sheet.

Core relationships

Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Equity includes contributed capital and RE, with treasury stock reducing total equity. Ending RE = beginning RE + NI - dividends. NI = revenues/gains - expenses/losses. Net sales - COGS = gross profit; subtract other operating expenses for operating income; include other items for pretax income; subtract tax for NI.

Balance sheet: at a date. Income statement, equity statement and cash-flow statement: over a period. Operating cash flows concern ordinary trade; investing concerns long-term productive assets and investments; financing concerns owners and lenders.

Account directions

Assets/expenses increase with debits. Liabilities/equity/revenues increase with credits. Decreases use the opposite side. Contra-assets (allowance and accumulated depreciation) normally credit; Treasury Stock and contra-revenues normally debit. In the class examples dividends directly debit RE.

Entry checklist: date → account names → account types → changes → sides → amounts → debits equal credits; credit lines below and indented.

The six timing patterns

Revenue: cash now, earned now    Dr Cash / Cr Revenue
Revenue: earned now, cash later  Dr A/R / Cr Revenue
Later collection                Dr Cash / Cr A/R
Revenue: cash first             Dr Cash / Cr Unearned Revenue
Later delivery                  Dr Unearned Revenue / Cr Revenue

Expense: incurred/paid now      Dr Expense / Cr Cash
Expense: incurred, cash later   Dr Expense / Cr Payable
Later settlement                Dr Payable / Cr Cash
Expense: prepay service         Dr Prepaid Asset / Cr Cash
Later use                       Dr Expense / Cr Prepaid Asset

Adjustments and closing

Adjustments: no Cash in the taught accrual/deferral entries. Expense used supplies = beginning + purchases - remaining. Interest = principal × annual rate × fraction of year. Depreciation: Dr Depreciation Expense / Cr Accumulated Depreciation. Net carrying amount = cost - accumulated depreciation. Straight-line annual depreciation, when specified = (cost - residual)/useful life.

EPS in the course examples = NI / weighted-average common shares outstanding; outstanding = issued - repurchased. Close revenue/gains: Dr Revenue/Gain, Cr RE. Close expense/losses: Dr RE, Cr Expense/Loss. Permanent balance-sheet accounts survive; temporary income-statement accounts reset.

Accounting cycle: analyze → journalize → post → unadjusted trial balance → adjust/post → adjusted trial balance → statements → close/post → post-closing trial balance.

Sales

FOB shipping point: transfer at shipment. FOB destination: transfer at delivery. Net sales = gross sales - discounts - returns/allowances. Apply a prompt-payment discount to the remaining eligible invoice after returns. Card fee is a separate operating expense in the class example: Dr Cash (net), Dr Fee Expense, Cr Sales (gross).

Bundle: contract → obligations → price → allocate → recognize each obligation when/as satisfied. Relative allocation = bundle price × item's standalone price / total standalone prices.

Receivables

Percentage of sales: expense = net credit sales × loss rate. Aging: target allowance = sum of age-group A/R × loss rates; expense adjustment = target credit balance - signed unadjusted credit balance (treat debit balance as negative). Net A/R = gross A/R - allowance.

Basic roll-forwards with no recoveries: ending gross A/R = beginning + net credit sales - collections - write-offs. Ending allowance = beginning credit + expense - write-offs. If recoveries occur, add reinstatements to both accounts and subtract collections from A/R.

Write-off       Dr Allowance / Cr A/R
Recovery        Dr A/R / Cr Allowance; then Dr Cash / Cr A/R
Bad debt estimate Dr Bad Debt Expense / Cr Allowance

A write-off changes gross A/R and allowance equally: net A/R and NI stay unchanged. Do not expense it twice.

Inventory

Net purchase cost includes freight-in/preparation and subtracts purchase returns/discounts. Available cost = beginning + net purchases = COGS + ending inventory. Periodic FIFO: oldest costs to COGS. Periodic LIFO: newest period costs to COGS. Weighted average: total available cost / total units; multiply by sold/remaining units. Specific identification uses the actual identified costs.

Rising costs: FIFO has lower COGS and higher income/inventory than LIFO; falling costs reverse this. Ending inventory overstated: current COGS understated and income overstated; next year reverses if next ending inventory is correct. Understatement reverses these directions. Ignore tax unless supplied.

Inventory supplement: NRV = sales price - costs to sell. Use the question's stated measurement basis and compare item by item when instructed; write-down entry in the course is Dr COGS / Cr Inventory. Perpetual calculations are excluded.
