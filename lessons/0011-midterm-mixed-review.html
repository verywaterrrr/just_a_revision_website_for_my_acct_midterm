ACCT2010 / Midterm / Lesson 08

Mixed review and exam readiness

About 40 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Retrieve and combine the course concepts before your independent past papers. This is a fresh mini-review, not a full-length mock or a prediction of exam questions.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. Work closed-book, then diagnose

Use paper, a calculator and about 40 minutes. For each answer, record confidence as high/medium/low before checking. A lucky correct answer still needs review. Classify errors as concept, timing, account, direction, arithmetic or presentation.

Use an adaptable 90-minute exam budget: about 50 minutes for 30 MCQs, 30 minutes for the two problems and 10 minutes for checks. This is a planning suggestion, not an exam rule. If stuck, mark the question and return; preserve time to show the problem-solving work.

2. Fast checks that catch costly mistakes

  • Journal entry: right date, account names, debit/credit directions, equal totals, indented credits.
  • Accrual: delivered/used now, paid before or after? Does the adjustment avoid Cash?
  • Receivables: expense target or ending-allowance target? Existing allowance debit or credit? Write-off already reflected?
  • Inventory: units reconcile; COGS + ending cost = total available cost; use purchase cost rather than selling price; periodic system.
  • Statements: current-period profit reaches RE once; dividends and share issues stay out of net income.

To decide readiness, aim for 90-95% on fresh mixed work and the ability to explain the answers. This is a practice target, not a known A+ grade boundary. Repeated correct results on changed questions are stronger evidence than rereading familiar solutions.

Try it without notes

1. Borrowing and the statements

Borrow $9,000 cash on a note. Give the journal entry, net income effect and cash-flow category.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Loan date
Dr Cash                       9,000
    Cr Notes Payable                   9,000

No net income effect. Financing inflow.

2. Revenue cut-off

Receive $4,800 on 1 November for six months of evenly provided services. Nothing after receipt is recorded. Give the 31 December adjustment and remaining liability.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Dec 31
Dr Unearned Revenue           1,600
    Cr Service Revenue                 1,600

Two months at $800; liability $3,200.

3. Accrued expense

Employees earn $900 before year-end, unpaid and unrecorded. Give the adjustment and the income/liability errors if omitted.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Dec 31
Dr Wages Expense                900
    Cr Wages Payable                     900

If omitted, liabilities understated $900 and income overstated $900.

4. Income and retained earnings

Revenue $32,000; expenses $23,000; dividends $2,000; beginning RE $6,000. Find NI and ending RE. Would a $5,000 share issue change either answer?

Reveal solution and reasoning

NI $9,000; ending RE $13,000. Share issue changes contributed capital, with neither NI nor RE affected.

5. Sales method versus aging

Net credit sales $80,000, estimated loss 2%; allowance before adjustment $300 credit. Find expense under percentage of sales, then under an alternative aging target of $1,600.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Sales method expense $1,600. Aging expense $1,300. These are alternative methods, not two adjustments to make together.

6. Contra-accounts

Classify Accumulated Depreciation, Treasury Stock and Sales Returns. State their normal balance and whether each survives closing.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Accumulated Depreciation: contra-asset, credit, permanent. Treasury Stock: contra-equity, debit, permanent. Sales Returns: contra-revenue, debit, temporary.

7. EPS

NI $18,000. Common shares outstanding: 4,000 for the first nine months, then 6,000 for three months. Find weighted-average shares and EPS.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Weighted average = 4,000×9/12 + 6,000×3/12 = 4,500. EPS = $4.

8. Inventory error

Ending inventory overstated $1,500 in Year 1. Year 2 ending inventory correct. State income errors in both years and explain using the COGS formula.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Year 1 income overstated $1,500 from excessive ending-inventory subtraction. Year 2 income understated $1,500 from excessive beginning inventory.

9. Integrated problem A: year-end accounts

Before adjustment: Service Revenue $20,000; Wages Expense $8,000; Cash $12,000; A/R $3,000; Equipment $10,000; Accumulated Depreciation $2,000 credit; Unearned Revenue $4,000; Common Stock $5,000; beginning RE $2,000. No dividends. At year-end: $1,000 of the advance is earned; $700 wages are unpaid/unrecorded; $500 depreciation is unrecorded. Prepare adjustments, net income, ending RE and the closing entries. Check the balance sheet.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Dec 31 adjustments
Dr Unearned Revenue           1,000
    Cr Service Revenue                 1,000
Dr Wages Expense                700
    Cr Wages Payable                     700
Dr Depreciation Expense         500
    Cr Accumulated Depreciation          500

Adjusted revenue $21,000; wages $8,700; depreciation $500. NI $11,800; ending RE $13,800. Assets = 12,000 + 3,000 + 10,000 - 2,500 = $22,500. Liabilities = 3,000 unearned + 700 wages = $3,700; equity = 5,000 + 13,800 = $18,800; total $22,500.

Dec 31 closing
Dr Service Revenue           21,000
    Cr Retained Earnings              21,000
Dr Retained Earnings          9,200
    Cr Wages Expense                   8,700
    Cr Depreciation Expense              500

10. Integrated problem B: receivables and inventory

Beginning A/R $12,000; credit sales $50,000; credit-sale returns $2,000; collections $44,000; write-offs $1,000. Beginning allowance $600 credit; ending aging estimate: 90% of ending A/R at 2% loss and 10% at 20% loss. No recoveries. Find gross/net A/R, allowance adjustment and write-off entry. Separately, inventory begins 10 units at $8; buy 20 at $11; sell 18; later buy 10 at $14. Find periodic FIFO and LIFO COGS/ending inventory.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Gross A/R = 12,000 + 50,000 - 2,000 - 44,000 - 1,000 = $15,000. Aging target = 13,500×2% + 1,500×20% = $570 credit. Unadjusted allowance $400 debit; expense $970; net A/R $14,430.

Write-off date
Dr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts 1,000
    Cr Accounts Receivable             1,000
Period-end adjustment
Dr Bad Debt Expense             970
    Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts   970

Inventory: available 40 units, cost 80 + 220 + 140 = $440; ending 22 units. FIFO COGS = 10×8 + 8×11 = $168; ending $272. LIFO COGS = 10×14 + 8×11 = $228; ending $212. Each pair reconciles to $440.
