ACCT2010 / Midterm / Lesson 03

When revenue and expenses happen

About 50 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Separate economic activity from cash movement. Learn all six revenue/expense timing cases and the income statement subtotals required by the guide.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. The operating cycle and time periods

The cash-to-cash operating cycle runs from purchasing resources, through selling goods/services, to collecting from customers. A business repeats the cycle continuously. The time-period assumption lets us report a month, quarter or year of that continuing activity; we must decide which revenues and expenses belong to that slice.

Cash-basis accounting records revenue when cash is received and expenses when cash is paid. Know what it means; the guide says you will not have to apply it. Accrual accounting records revenue when goods/services are delivered and expenses when resources or services are consumed/incurred, regardless of the payment date.

The revenue principle also requires the amount the business expects to be entitled to receive. The matching principle records costs in the period of the related benefits/revenue. Unused resources remain assets; services already received can create an expense and a liability.

Advance receipt date

    Cr Unearned Revenue                1,200

Dr Unearned Revenue           1,200
    Cr Service Revenue                 1,200

Accounts Receivable means the customer owes us money for earned revenue. Unearned Revenue means we owe the customer future delivery after an advance payment. When we collect an existing receivable, revenue has already been recorded. When we fulfil an advance-funded service, the liability falls and revenue rises.

Unpaid wages belong to the second expense case. Employees worked for us → expense incurred. We still owe payment → payable. Customer revenue accounts have no role in that transaction.

For prepaid services use Prepaid Rent/Insurance. For a tangible resource, use its asset name: Supplies, Inventory or Equipment. Later consumption creates the corresponding expense. Purchases and expenses can fall in different periods.

4. Read a multi-step income statement

  • Operating revenues minus operating expenses = income from operations.
  • Add/subtract other items = income before income taxes / pretax income.
  • Subtract income tax expense = net income.

For a merchandiser, net sales minus COGS gives gross profit before the other operating expenses. In the Chapter 3 examples, interest revenue/expense and investment disposal gains/losses are other items. A gain/loss equals proceeds minus the disposed asset's carrying amount. A loss is the negative difference.

The course's Chipotle example includes PPE disposal gains/losses in operating expenses. Follow a question's presentation and the relevant class example; the word 'gain' alone does not determine the subtotal.

Calculate the subtotals

Service revenue $50,000; wages/rent/utilities expenses $30,000; interest revenue $500; interest expense $1,500; income tax expense $3,800.

Operating income = $50,000 - $30,000 = $20,000. Pretax income = $20,000 + $500 - $1,500 = $19,000. Net income = $19,000 - $3,800 = $15,200.

December income decreases $2,800. January settlement creates no new wage expense.

2. Before and after delivery

On 1 November collect $6,000 for services provided evenly from November through January. Record receipt and the 31 December adjustment, assuming nothing else was recorded. How much liability remains?

Reveal solution and reasoning
Nov 1
Dr Cash                       6,000
    Cr Unearned Revenue                6,000
Dec 31
Dr Unearned Revenue           4,000
    Cr Service Revenue                 4,000

Two months earned at $2,000/month. Remaining liability $2,000.

3. Avoid double counting

Collect $9,000 from a customer: $5,000 settles existing A/R and $4,000 pays for services delivered today. Write the entry. How much current revenue?

Reveal solution and reasoning
Collection / service date
Dr Cash                       9,000
    Cr Accounts Receivable             5,000
    Cr Service Revenue                 4,000

Current revenue $4,000; the earlier $5,000 revenue was already recognized.

4. Calculate income

Revenue $80,000; operating expenses $52,000; interest revenue $600; interest expense $1,600; tax expense $5,400. Find operating, pretax and net income.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Operating $28,000; pretax $27,000; net income $21,600. Tax follows pretax income.
