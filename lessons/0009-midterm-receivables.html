Receivables and bad debts

About 65 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Use the allowance method confidently, including credit/debit allowance balances, write-offs, recoveries and net receivables. The guide explicitly identifies this as Chapter 6’s focus.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. Recognize the expected collection loss

A receivable is the right to collect money. A/R normally arises from sales on account; Notes Receivable involve a formal note, often with interest. A customer may fail to pay: that collection loss is bad debt. The allowance method estimates losses in the period of the related sales and later removes specific uncollectible accounts.

Period end: estimate collection losses $2,000
Dr Bad Debt Expense           2,000
    Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts 2,000

Bad Debt Expense reduces current profit. Allowance for Doubtful Accounts is a contra-asset with a normal credit balance. It reduces the reported receivable, without identifying which particular customer will fail.

Net A/R = Gross A/R - Allowance for Doubtful Accounts

Gross A/R $50,000 and allowance $2,000 means net A/R $48,000. The allowance is an estimate of uncollectibility, not a cash reserve or a liability.

2. Percentage of net credit sales: find expense directly

Compute the relevant net credit sales, then multiply by the estimated loss rate. This produces Bad Debt Expense for the period, regardless of the allowance's existing balance. Post that expense and calculate the resulting allowance balance if requested.

Sales-based estimate

Gross credit sales $200,000; credit-sale returns $10,000; credit-sale discounts $2,000; estimated loss rate 2%. Net credit sales = $188,000. Bad Debt Expense = $188,000 × 2% = $3,760.

Record Dr Bad Debt Expense $3,760 / Cr Allowance $3,760. If the allowance already has a $500 credit balance after write-offs, it ends at $4,260 credit. Do not subtract $500 from the calculated expense.

3. Aging: find the ending allowance first

  1. Multiply each age group's ending gross A/R by its loss rate; add the estimates. This gives the required ending allowance.
  2. Find the allowance's unadjusted balance after the period's write-offs/recoveries.
  3. Calculate the adjustment needed to reach the target. Credit balance already there: subtract it. Debit balance: add its magnitude.

Credit balance and debit balance variants

Ending receivables: $30,000 current × 1% = $300; $8,000 overdue × 5% = $400; $2,000 very overdue × 25% = $500. Target allowance = $1,200 credit.

  • Unadjusted allowance $300 credit → expense $1,200 - $300 = $900.
  • Unadjusted allowance $300 debit → expense $1,200 + $300 = $1,500.

Both adjustments end at the same $1,200 credit target. Gross A/R $40,000 less allowance $1,200 = $38,800 net A/R.

If an existing credit allowance exceeds the required target, the adjustment reduces it: debit Allowance and credit Bad Debt Expense for the excess. Use the target balance to determine the direction.

4. Reconstruct both T-accounts

Ending gross A/R = Beginning A/R + Net credit sales - Collections - Write-offs
Ending allowance (credit) = Beginning allowance (credit) + Expense - Write-offs

These are the guide's basic formulas when there are no recoveries or other adjustments. Thus, aging expense = desired ending allowance - beginning allowance + write-offs. If an unadjusted allowance balance is already given, write-offs are already reflected; subtract that signed balance directly instead of adding write-offs again.

Find the missing expense from a roll-forward

Beginning allowance $5,000 credit, write-offs $6,000, aging target $4,000 credit. Unadjusted allowance = $5,000 - $6,000 = $1,000 debit. Expense $5,000 brings it to $4,000 credit.

Allowance for Doubtful Accounts
Debit (left)        | Credit (right)
Write-offs 6,000     | Beginning 5,000
                    | Expense   5,000
Ending credit balance = 4,000

5. Write off a specific customer

Write-off date: a $700 account is uncollectible
Dr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts 700
    Cr Accounts Receivable               700

Gross A/R and the allowance both decrease $700. Their difference, net A/R, stays unchanged. The write-off creates no new Bad Debt Expense under the allowance method. Example: $50,000 - $2,000 = $48,000 before; $49,300 - $1,300 = $48,000 after. Cash and net income also remain unchanged.

6. Recover a previously written-off account

First reinstate the receivable and allowance; then record the collection. For a $700 recovery:

Recovery date: reinstate
Dr Accounts Receivable          700
    Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts   700
Recovery date: collect
Dr Cash                         700
    Cr Accounts Receivable               700

The reinstatement increases both gross A/R and allowance. Collection exchanges the receivable for cash. Across both entries there is no new revenue or expense; total net assets stay unchanged. When recoveries occur, include reinstatements as increases in the A/R and allowance roll-forwards, and collections in A/R's decreases.

8. Brief slide supplement: cash and controls

Cash includes money and instruments banks accept for immediate deposit/credit. Cash equivalents are readily convertible, low-risk investments with original maturities of three months or less. A long-term investment merely approaching maturity does not satisfy the original-maturity condition. Cash management protects cash, supplies operating needs and avoids excess idle cash. A/R controls include independent credit approval, aging and following up overdue balances. The provided slides mention bank reconciliations as a control but do not develop a reconciliation calculation here.

Try it without notes

1. Choose the method

Net credit sales $100,000; loss rate 3%; existing allowance $900 credit. What is the expense using percentage of sales? What would expense be if an aging schedule instead required a $3,000 ending allowance?

Reveal solution and reasoning

Sales method: expense $3,000; ending allowance $3,900. Aging: expense $2,100; ending allowance $3,000. The same $3,000 has a different meaning under each method.

2. Aging debit balance

Required allowance $6,400 credit; unadjusted allowance $600 debit. Calculate and record the adjustment.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Period end
Dr Bad Debt Expense           7,000
    Cr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts 7,000

A $7,000 credit first eliminates the $600 debit and then establishes the $6,400 credit.

3. Full roll-forward

Beginning gross A/R $30,000; credit sales $90,000; credit-sale returns $5,000; collections $80,000; write-offs $2,000. Beginning allowance $1,500 credit; aging target $2,500 credit; no recoveries. Find ending gross A/R, expense and net A/R.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Gross A/R = 30,000 + 90,000 - 5,000 - 80,000 - 2,000 = 33,000. Unadjusted allowance = 1,500 - 2,000 = 500 debit; expense 3,000. Net A/R = 33,000 - 2,500 = 30,500.

4. Write-off effect

Gross A/R $20,000, allowance $1,000 credit. Write off a $400 account. Give the entry, gross A/R, allowance, net A/R and income effect.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Write-off date
Dr Allowance for Doubtful Accounts 400
    Cr Accounts Receivable               400

Gross $19,600; allowance $600 credit; net $19,000 unchanged; no current income effect.
