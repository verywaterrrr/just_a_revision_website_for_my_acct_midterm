ACCT2010 / Midterm / Lesson 05

Sales, discounts and bundled revenue

About 40 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Recognize sales on the correct date and calculate what the seller actually earns after returns and discounts. Keep card fees separate from contra-revenue.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. Delivery controls recognition

For services, recognize revenue as the service is provided. For goods, the course ties recognition to transfer of title/risks under shipping terms.

  • FOB shipping point: ownership transfers at shipment. The buyer includes goods in transit in its inventory; the seller records the sale at shipment.
  • FOB destination: ownership transfers at delivery. Goods in transit still belong to the seller; the seller waits until delivery to record the sale.

Goods ship 29 December and arrive 3 January. Shipping point: seller's December sale and buyer's December inventory. Destination: seller's December inventory and January sale. Identify whether you are the buyer or seller before deciding the effect.

2. Three forms of sale

A $1,000 cash sale debits Cash $1,000 and credits Sales Revenue $1,000. A $1,000 credit sale debits A/R $1,000 and credits Sales Revenue $1,000; the customer pays later. For a card sale in the course's immediate-settlement model, the card company deducts its fee and deposits the net amount.

Sale date: $1,000 card sale; 2% fee
Dr Cash                         980
Dr Credit Card Fee Expense       20
    Cr Sales Revenue                   1,000

Record the full $1,000 revenue and a separate operating expense. The fee does not reduce net sales under this class treatment. The guide's credit sales create customer A/R; the card example does not create customer A/R.

3. Sales discounts, returns and allowances

2/10, n/30 means a 2% discount if the customer pays within ten days; otherwise the full amount is due in thirty days. Record the initial credit sale at gross amount. When a qualifying payment arrives, debit Cash for what is received and Sales Discounts for the reduction, and credit A/R for the full balance settled.

A return gives goods back; an allowance reduces the price when goods are retained. Both use the debit-balance contra-revenue account Sales Returns and Allowances. For an unpaid invoice credit A/R; for a refund of an already paid sale credit Cash. This lesson isolates the revenue side; inventory costing is in Lesson 7.

A return before a discounted payment

On 1 October sell $10,000 on account, terms 2/10, n/30. The customer returns $2,000 on 4 October and pays the remaining balance on 8 October. Discount applies to the $8,000 kept.

Oct 1
Dr Accounts Receivable       10,000
    Cr Sales Revenue                  10,000
Oct 4
Dr Sales Returns and Allowances 2,000
    Cr Accounts Receivable             2,000
Oct 8
Dr Cash                       7,840
Dr Sales Discounts              160
    Cr Accounts Receivable             8,000

Net sales = $10,000 - $2,000 - $160 = $7,840. If payment occurs after the discount window, debit Cash $8,000 and credit A/R $8,000, with no discount.

Net sales = Gross sales (all sales forms) - Sales discounts - Sales returns and allowances

Only subtract a credit-sale return/discount when calculating net credit sales for bad debt estimation. Do not subtract cash-sale returns a second time or include unrelated card fees in that base.

4. Bundled sales: split the promises

  1. Identify the contract.
  2. Identify each distinct performance obligation (promised good/service).
  3. Determine the transaction price.
  4. Allocate the price to the obligations.
  5. Recognize each portion when/as its obligation is satisfied.

If standalone selling prices are given for allocating a discount, use each obligation's relative share: bundle price × individual standalone price / total standalone prices. If the question already supplies allocated amounts, use them directly.

Hardware now, support later

A device's standalone price is $900 and twelve months of support is $300. Sell the bundle for $1,080 and deliver the device on 1 October; support starts that day and is provided evenly.

Allocate $1,080 × 900/1,200 = $810 hardware and $1,080 × 300/1,200 = $270 support. At sale, $810 is earned and $270 is deferred. By 31 December, three months of support earns $67.50; total revenue to date $877.50 and remaining liability $202.50.

Oct 1
Dr Cash                       1,080
    Cr Hardware Revenue                  810
    Cr Unearned Support Revenue          270
Dec 31
Dr Unearned Support Revenue    67.50
    Cr Support Revenue                 67.50

The slides' iPad example has $450 hardware and $50 upgrades over five years. It earns $450 at delivery and $10 per full year of evenly supplied upgrades: first full year $460, later years $10 each.

5. Brief slide supplement: taking a discount

A 2% discount saves $2 by paying $98 twenty days earlier under 2/10, n/30. Forgoing it costs 2/98 over twenty days; simple annualized equivalent = (2/98) × (365/20) ≈ 37.24%. This illustrates the incentive in the slides; prioritize recognizing and recording the actual discount.

Try it without notes

1. Apply the shipping date

A seller ships goods on 30 December FOB destination. They arrive 4 January. Who owns them on 31 December, and in which month does the seller recognize revenue?

Reveal solution and reasoning

The seller owns them on 31 December. Revenue is recognized in January at delivery.

2. Combine return and discount

Credit sale $5,000, terms 3/10, n/30; $1,000 returned before payment; customer pays on day 8. Calculate cash received and net sales; write the collection entry.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Day 8
Dr Cash                       3,880
Dr Sales Discounts              120
    Cr Accounts Receivable             4,000

Discount = $4,000 × 3% = $120. Cash and net sales are both $3,880 for this isolated transaction after the return.

3. Card fee versus discount

Card sales $2,000 with a 3% fee, cash sales $1,000, credit sales $5,000, sales returns $400 and sales discounts $100. Calculate net sales and the card-fee expense.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Gross sales $8,000; net sales $7,500. Card fee expense $60, recorded separately. Net card cash receipt is $1,940.

4. Bundle timing

A $600 cash bundle has allocated prices of $480 for a device and $120 for twelve months of service. Device delivered and service begins 1 December. Find December revenue and 31 December unearned revenue.

Reveal solution and reasoning

December revenue $480 + $120/12 = $490. Unearned service revenue $110.
