ACCT2010 / Midterm / Lesson 04

Adjustments, statements and closing

About 60 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Bring the accounts to the correct period-end amounts, calculate EPS, connect adjusted balances to statements, and reset temporary accounts.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. Why adjustments exist

A period can end between cash movement and delivery/use. Adjustments recognize the current period's unrecorded revenues and expenses and update the related asset/liability. In the course's accrual/deferral adjustments, Cash is absent: its receipt/payment was recorded earlier or happens later. Each entry includes an income-statement account and a balance-sheet account.

For every adjustment: (1) identify earned revenue or incurred expense; (2) find the existing/future asset or liability; (3) calculate only the amount belonging to the period; (4) record and check the resulting balance.

2. The four timing categories

  • Accrued revenue: earned, unrecorded, cash later → Dr Receivable, Cr Revenue.
  • Deferred revenue: cash earlier, now earned → Dr Unearned Revenue, Cr Revenue.
  • Accrued expense: incurred, unrecorded, cash later → Dr Expense, Cr Payable.
  • Deferred expense: cash earlier, benefit now used → Dr Expense, Cr Prepaid asset/Supplies (or the depreciation contra-asset).

Prepaid insurance and supplies

On 1 October pay $2,400 for twelve months of insurance and record Prepaid Insurance. By 31 December, 3/12 × $2,400 = $600 has expired; prepaid insurance remaining = $1,800.

Dec 31
Dr Insurance Expense            600
    Cr Prepaid Insurance                 600

Supplies begin at $300; purchases $900; physical supplies left $250. Available $1,200 - remaining $250 = $950 used.

Dec 31
Dr Supplies Expense             950
    Cr Supplies                          950

Unpaid interest and tax

A $12,000 note bears 6% annual interest. Three months have elapsed without recording or paying interest: $12,000 × 0.06 × 3/12 = $180. If income tax incurred is separately given as $700 and unpaid, accrue it too.

Dec 31
Dr Interest Expense             180
    Cr Interest Payable                  180
Dr Income Tax Expense           700
    Cr Income Tax Payable                700

3. Depreciation and contra-assets

Depreciation allocates a productive asset's cost over the periods of use. Land is not depreciated in these examples. The expense describes this period's allocated cost. Accumulated Depreciation is the total allocation to date, a permanent contra-asset with a credit balance.

If annual depreciation is given as $2,400, a quarter's expense is $600. If a straight-line problem gives cost $12,000, residual value $2,000 and life five years, annual depreciation is ($12,000 - $2,000)/5 = $2,000. Use only the months in service specified by the question.

Dec 31, quarterly adjustment
Dr Depreciation Expense         600
    Cr Accumulated Depreciation          600
Asset carrying amount = Recorded cost - Accumulated Depreciation

Show Equipment at $12,000, less Accumulated Depreciation $3,000, net $9,000. Keep the asset's cost account separate from its accumulated allocation. The contra-asset appears in the asset section; it is not a liability.

4. Predict an omitted adjustment

Unrecorded accrued wages: expenses and liabilities too low; profit and equity too high. Unrecorded accrued service revenue: assets and revenue too low; profit and equity too low. Unrecorded expired insurance: assets too high and expenses too low; profit/equity too high. Unrecorded earned advance revenue: liability too high and revenue too low; profit/equity too low. Omitted depreciation: accumulated depreciation too low, net assets and profit too high.

Derive the effects from the missing debit and credit rather than memorizing unrelated arrows. Ignore tax effects unless the question includes them.

5. From adjusted trial balance to statements

The guide's first page excludes full statement preparation, but its Chapter 4 list includes it. This short example covers the mechanics as a precaution. Income-statement and equity balances remain unclosed in the adjusted trial balance.

A complete miniature statement walkthrough

Adjusted balances: Cash $8,000; A/R $2,000; Equipment $10,000; Accumulated Depreciation $2,000 credit; A/P $3,000; Common Stock $8,000; beginning RE $4,000; Service Revenue $9,000; Wages Expense $4,000; Rent Expense $1,000; Depreciation Expense $1,000. No dividends or new share issues this period. Debits and credits each total $26,000.

  1. Income statement: $9,000 - $4,000 - $1,000 - $1,000 = $3,000 net income.
  2. Equity statement: RE $4,000 + $3,000 = $7,000; Common Stock $8,000; total equity $15,000.
  3. Balance sheet: current assets $10,000; equipment net $8,000; total assets $18,000. Liabilities $3,000 + equity $15,000 = $18,000.

Use ending RE $7,000 in the balance sheet. The adjusted-trial-balance RE $4,000 has not yet received this period's income. Do not add the income twice.

6. Earnings per share

EPS = Net income / Weighted-average common shares outstanding

This is the formula shown in the supplied course slide; no preferred shares are involved in these examples. Outstanding shares = issued shares - repurchased shares. If shares change during the year, weight each outstanding count by the fraction of the year it applied.

Example: NI $30,000; 10,000 shares outstanding January-June, then 14,000 July-December. Weighted average = 10,000 × 6/12 + 14,000 × 6/12 = 12,000 shares. EPS = $2.50 per share. If the count stays constant, use that count.

7. Closing and the accounting cycle

Permanent (real/stock) accounts carry balances forward: assets, contra-assets, liabilities and equity. Temporary (nominal/flow) accounts measure a period: revenue, gains, expenses and losses. Close them to zero and transfer net income/loss to RE. RE itself continues into the next period.

Period end: revenue 9,000; expenses total 6,000
Dr Service Revenue            9,000
    Cr Retained Earnings               9,000
Dr Retained Earnings          6,000
    Cr Wages Expense                   4,000
    Cr Rent Expense                    1,000
    Cr Depreciation Expense            1,000

RE increases by net income $3,000. Accumulated Depreciation remains; only Depreciation Expense closes. The course records dividends directly against RE, so no extra dividend-account closing entry is needed for those examples.

Cycle: analyze → journalize → post to ledger → unadjusted trial balance → adjust and post → adjusted trial balance → financial statements → close and post → post-closing trial balance. The last trial balance contains only permanent accounts. Adjusting entries recognize performance; closing entries transfer the already recognized performance.

Try it without notes

1. Build the adjustments

At year-end: $3,600 prepaid rent covers 1 November through 30 April; supplies available total $1,100 with $180 left; wages $750 are incurred and unpaid. All prepayments/purchases were recorded as assets. Prepare three adjustments.

Reveal solution and reasoning
Dec 31
Dr Rent Expense               1,200
    Cr Prepaid Rent                    1,200
Dr Supplies Expense             920
    Cr Supplies                          920
Dr Wages Expense                750
    Cr Wages Payable                     750

Rent: two of six months used. Supplies: available minus remaining. Wages: incurred now, cash later.

2. Explain omission effects

What happens to assets, liabilities and net income if $800 of earned advance revenue is not adjusted? Assume no tax effects.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Missing entry: Dr Unearned Revenue 800, Cr Revenue 800. Assets unchanged; liabilities overstated 800; income and equity understated 800.

3. EPS and closing

NI $24,000; 8,000 shares all year. Calculate EPS. Which survive closing: A/R, Sales Revenue, Wages Expense, Accumulated Depreciation and Retained Earnings?

Reveal solution and reasoning

EPS $3. A/R, Accumulated Depreciation and RE survive. Sales Revenue and Wages Expense reset to zero.

4. Separate adjustment from closing

An annual insurance policy of $1,200 starts 1 December. Prepare the year-end adjustment. Would closing Insurance Expense reverse that reduction of the prepaid asset?

Reveal solution and reasoning
Dec 31 adjustment
Dr Insurance Expense            100
    Cr Prepaid Insurance                 100
Dec 31 closing (this expense only)
Dr Retained Earnings            100
    Cr Insurance Expense                 100

Prepaid Insurance remains $1,100. Closing transfers the expense to RE and leaves the adjusted asset intact.
