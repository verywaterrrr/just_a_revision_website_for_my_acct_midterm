ACCT2010 / Midterm / Lesson 07

Inventory and cost of goods sold

About 75 minutes including written practice. Tutor-created examples unless identified as slide examples.

Calculate inventory and COGS under periodic specific identification, FIFO, LIFO and weighted average; explain price and error effects over two periods.

Read one section, look away, and explain it aloud. For questions, write your answer before revealing the solution. The scratch boxes do not save; keep important workings on paper or send them in chat.

1. What belongs in inventory

Inventory is tangible property held for sale or used in producing goods/services for sale. Merchandisers hold merchandise inventory. Manufacturers hold raw materials, work in process and finished goods. It is a current asset; the cost becomes COGS when the related inventory is sold.

Inventory cost includes costs necessary to bring goods to saleable condition and location: invoice price, freight-in, inspection and preparation, less purchase returns/allowances and purchase discounts. Ordinary delivery to customers is a selling cost rather than freight-in. Example: invoice $10,000 + freight-in $600 + preparation $200 - purchase returns $1,000 - purchase discount $180 = $9,620 inventory acquisition cost.

Ownership matters at the count date. For goods in transit, FOB shipping point transfers ownership at shipment; FOB destination at arrival. Include goods the company owns even if they have not physically arrived. Exclude shipped goods once ownership has passed to the customer.

2. One cost pool, two destinations

Beginning inventory + Net purchase cost = Goods available for sale
Goods available for sale = Ending inventory + COGS

The same relationship applies to units: beginning units + purchased units - sold units = ending units (absent losses/returns). Calculate units before assigning costs. Selling price determines revenue; purchase/unit costs determine inventory and COGS.

Example: beginning inventory $8,000, net purchases $32,000, ending inventory $9,000 → available $40,000 and COGS $31,000. If COGS is instead given, rearrange: ending inventory = beginning + purchases - COGS.

3. Periodic is the midterm system

Periodic costing allocates the whole period's available goods at period end. For the cost-flow calculations, include all purchases for the period, even a purchase after a sale. Perpetual updates layers after each sale and is explicitly excluded from this midterm.

Use this common pool for three methods: beginning 2 units at $70; buy 4 at $80; sell 4 for $120 each; later buy 1 at $100. Total available 7 units costing $560. Sold 4; ending 3. Sales revenue is $480 under all three methods.

4. FIFO: oldest costs go to COGS

Assign the earliest 4 costs to the units sold: 2 × $70 + 2 × $80 = COGS $300. Remaining costs are 2 × $80 + 1 × $100 = ending inventory $260. Check $300 + $260 = $560.

5. LIFO: newest costs go to COGS

Periodic LIFO uses the latest purchase for the period, even though it followed the sale: 1 × $100 + 3 × $80 = COGS $340. Ending inventory is 2 × $70 + 1 × $80 = $220. Check $340 + $220 = $560.

6. Weighted average: blend unit costs

Average unit cost = Total available cost / Total available units

$560 / 7 = $80 per unit. COGS 4 × $80 = $320; ending inventory 3 × $80 = $240. Weight by the units in each batch; a simple average of $70, $80 and $100 would be wrong because the batches have different sizes. Keep sufficient precision until the final answer unless the question gives a rounding rule.

7. Specific identification

Track the actual identified cost of each item sold. If a jeweller sells identified pieces costing $200 and $500, COGS is $700 even if another piece costing $900 remains. This suits unique high-value items. FIFO/LIFO/average are cost-flow assumptions and need not describe the physical order goods leave the shelf.

A fresh pool: solve all three

Beginning 10 units at $4; purchases 10 units at $6; 12 sold. Available 20 units/$100; ending 8 units.

  • FIFO: COGS 10 × 4 + 2 × 6 = $52; ending 8 × 6 = $48.
  • LIFO: COGS 10 × 6 + 2 × 4 = $68; ending 8 × 4 = $32.
  • Average: $100/20 = $5; COGS $60; ending $40.

After solving FIFO, try LIFO and average to check whether you can transfer the same logic.

8. Rising versus falling costs

Holding selling revenue and other costs constant, with rising purchase costs: FIFO assigns cheap older costs to COGS, so it gives lower COGS, higher gross profit/net income and higher ending inventory than LIFO. Weighted average falls between them in the straightforward rising-cost examples. LIFO gives higher COGS and lower income/inventory.

With falling purchase costs, reverse those comparisons: FIFO has higher COGS and lower income/inventory; LIFO has lower COGS and higher income/inventory. Tax expense follows taxable income only under the assumptions supplied by a question; the slides discuss why lower reported taxable profit may be attractive.

For the supplied slides' standards comparison: US GAAP permits FIFO, LIFO and average; IFRS prohibits LIFO. Use the permitted method specified in the exam exercise. Consistency means using the method from year to year and explaining a justified change. A jurisdiction's restriction does not remove LIFO calculations from this guide's scope.

9. Inventory errors across two years

Start from COGS = beginning inventory + purchases - ending inventory. An ending-inventory overstatement makes the subtraction too large, so current COGS is too low and current gross profit/pretax income too high. Assets and ending RE are too high, ignoring tax effects.

That wrong ending inventory becomes next year's wrong beginning inventory. If next year's ending inventory is correct, next year's COGS is too high and its income too low by the same amount. The two annual income errors offset cumulatively; next year's ending inventory and cumulative RE are correct after the normal year-end process, assuming no other errors and ignoring taxes.

Correct two reported profits

Year 1 reported pretax income $50,000; Year 2 $60,000. Year 1 ending inventory was overstated $4,000 and Year 2 ending inventory was correct.

Correct Year 1 income = $50,000 - $4,000 = $46,000. Correct Year 2 income = $60,000 + $4,000 = $64,000. Combined income stays $110,000. An understatement reverses these directions.

10. Slide supplement: inventory write-down

This is in the slides but not expressly named in the guide's inventory list. Inventory carried at cost may need a write-down when the question's prescribed recoverable/market measure is lower. NRV = selling price - costs to sell. If cost is $100 and selling price $90 with $5 selling cost, NRV is $85; write down $15.

Try it without notes

1. All three periodic methods

Beginning 5 units at $10; buy 10 at $13; sell 9 units; later buy 5 at $16. Calculate available cost, ending units and COGS/ending inventory under FIFO, LIFO and weighted average.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Available 20 units, cost $50 + $130 + $80 = $260; ending 11 units. FIFO COGS 5×10 + 4×13 = $102; ending $158. LIFO COGS 5×16 + 4×13 = $132; ending $128. Average $13/unit: COGS $117; ending $143. Each pair sums to $260.

2. Ownership and costs

A buyer counts $20,000 on hand; goods costing $3,000 shipped to it FOB shipping point have not arrived; another $4,000 shipped to it FOB destination has not arrived. Calculate inventory before other adjustments. Is $500 freight-in part of inventory acquisition cost?

Reveal solution and reasoning

Inventory $23,000: include the $3,000 shipping-point goods and exclude the $4,000 destination goods still owned by their seller. Freight-in is included in acquisition cost.

3. Reverse the price trend

Purchase costs decline steadily. Which has higher COGS and ending inventory: FIFO or LIFO? Give the reason.

Reveal solution and reasoning

FIFO has higher COGS because it sends older, higher costs to expense. LIFO has higher ending inventory because it leaves the older, higher costs in inventory.

4. Two-year error

Year 1 ending inventory is understated $2,000; Year 2 ending inventory is correct. Describe COGS and income errors in each year, and the cumulative RE effect at the end of Year 2. Ignore tax.

Reveal solution and reasoning

Year 1 COGS overstated and income understated $2,000. Year 2 beginning inventory understated, COGS understated and income overstated $2,000. The cumulative RE error is zero at the end of Year 2.

5. Item-by-item write-down

Item A: 10 units, cost $20/unit, NRV $17/unit. Item B: 5 units, cost $30/unit, NRV $35/unit. Apply lower of cost and NRV item by item and record the write-down.

Reveal solution and reasoning

A = $170; B = $150; total inventory $320, compared with original cost $350. Dr COGS $30 / Cr Inventory $30. B’s higher NRV creates no gain.
