Your accounting course

Learn the full named guide scope through eight focused lessons, then test yourself independently with past papers.

Start with the foundations, then practise journal entries, accruals, receivables and inventory. Each lesson includes worked examples and hidden-answer questions.

  1. Start at Lesson 1. Read one short section and explain it without looking.
  2. Work the fresh questions on paper or in the scratch boxes before revealing the answers.
  3. Send your answers in chat. We will diagnose mistakes, change the numbers and retest before moving on.
  4. Return to earlier weak topics after another lesson and again the following day. Keep past papers untouched until the taught revision is complete.

Every required concept has a lesson

This is a coverage map of teaching material. It is not a prediction of question weights or a record of your mastery. Open a chapter below to jump to the exact teaching section.

Chapter 1 · Guide pp. 1-2
  • General function of accounting; financial versus managerial accounting; stakeholders and their decisions; GAAP/FASB and IFRS/IASB.
  • Operating, investing and financing activities; classify the associated cash flows.
  • Assets, liabilities and equity; accounting equation; typical account names.
  • Four basic statements, their elements and time periods; revenue, expenses and net income; contributed capital and retained earnings; issuance, repurchases and dividends; relationships among statements.
Chapter 2 · Guide p. 2
  • Separate entity, going concern, monetary unit and historical cost; classified balance-sheet elements.
  • Transaction, account, journal entry, general journal and general ledger.
  • Debit/credit rules, normal balances and account changes.
  • All eight class investing/financing transaction types: stock issuance including par/APIC, borrowing, asset acquisition, mixed cash/lease financing, investment sale, principal repayment, repurchase and dividend declaration/payment.
  • Post entries to T-accounts; date, debit-first format, indented credits and equality; trial balance.
Chapter 3 · Guide pp. 2-3
  • Operating (cash-to-cash) cycle; time-period assumption; cash versus accrual accounting.
  • Multi-step income statement: operating revenues/expenses, other items, operating income, pretax income and net income.
  • Revenue principle and entries when cash arrives before, at or after earning.
  • Matching principle and entries when cash is paid before, at or after incurrence; prepaid services versus tangible assets.
  • All eleven class operating patterns, including prior-period settlements, mixed cash/credit sales, gift cards, land disposal and interest.
Chapter 4 · Guide pp. 3-4
  • Why adjusting entries are needed at period end; no Cash in the taught adjustments.
  • All four accrual/deferral adjustment categories; supplies, prepayments, interest, wages and tax.
  • Depreciation, accumulated depreciation as a contra-asset, and net presentation beneath the asset.
  • Permanent/real/stock versus temporary/nominal/flow accounts; reset temporary accounts; close revenues/gains and expenses/losses to RE; full accounting cycle.
  • EPS definition and formula; weighted-average shares outstanding.
  • Adjusted-trial-balance statement preparation walkthrough, retained as a precaution because the guide conflicts.
Chapter 6 · Guide pp. 4-6
  • Goods/service revenue recognition; FOB shipping point and FOB destination.
  • Cash, credit-card and credit sales; record credit-card fee expense.
  • Gross sales, net sales, sales discounts and sales returns/allowances; calculate and record them.
  • Bundled sales: identify obligations, allocate transaction price and recognize on fulfilment.
Chapter 6 allowance focus · Guide pp. 5-6
  • Bad debt and the allowance method; allowance is a contra-asset; present net A/R.
  • Percentage of net credit sales: compute net credit sales, expense and entry.
  • Aging: compute required ending allowance, derive expense and record adjustment.
  • Reconstruct gross A/R and allowance using beginning balances, credit sales, collections and actual write-offs.
  • Write off actual uncollectible A/R; explain net receivable and income effects.
  • Slide supplement: recoveries and entries.
  • Slide supplement: cash/cash equivalents and basic controls.
  • Source discrepancy: Chapter 6 p. 54 question versus p. 55 answer.
Chapter 7 · Guide pp. 6-7
  • Inventory definition and components; costs included in inventory; ownership in transit.
  • Calculate available goods, COGS and ending inventory in dollars and units; solve any missing item.
  • Specific identification and cost-flow assumptions.
  • Periodic FIFO calculations.
  • Periodic LIFO calculations.
  • Periodic weighted-average calculations.
  • Effects of methods during rising and declining prices; brief slide standards comparison.
  • Ending-inventory errors in the current and following periods.
  • Periodic focus; perpetual calculations explicitly excluded.
  • Slide supplement: inventory write-downs using the measurement basis stated in each question.
